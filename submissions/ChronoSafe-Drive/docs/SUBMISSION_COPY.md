## Short abstract

```text
ChronoSafe-Drive is an end-to-end autonomous-driving safety pipeline built on AMD Radeon GPUs and ROCm. It combines physics-based MetaDrive simulation with public nuScenes camera/LiDAR streams, diagnoses camera, LiDAR, and timing health from causal temporal evidence, and predicts a stress-calibrated upper risk bound. A frozen three-state controller converts this risk into NORMAL, DEGRADED, and MINIMAL_RISK behavior. Timestamp-authenticity augmentation improves severe timestamp-corruption accuracy by 11.38 to 27.32 percentage points while retaining 99.34% intact accuracy. Across 20 unseen validated-Xvfb MetaDrive runs, the system achieves 100% minimal-risk-maneuver success, 0% premature triggers, 97.07% diagnosis accuracy, and 99.83% upper-risk coverage. On an AMD Radeon gfx1100 GPU with ROCm 7.2, the 56,968-parameter model provides sub-millisecond inference and up to 1.37 million temporal sequences per second.
```

## Three innovations

```text
1. Factorized temporal self-diagnosis: a shared eight-frame GRU with independent camera, LiDAR, and timing heads preserves the compositional structure of compound sensor degradation.

2. Timestamp-authenticity robustness with stress conformal calibration: missing, drifting, jittered, swapped, and spoofed timestamps are treated as fallible inputs, and the worst held-out calibration domain defines a conservative upper-risk radius.

3. Calibration-only minimal-risk control: a threshold selected only on two calibration seeds drives a hysteretic NORMAL-DEGRADED-MINIMAL_RISK controller, then remains frozen across 20 unseen closed-loop seeds.
```

## Application value

```text
ChronoSafe-Drive targets fail-safe autonomous mobility. Its health monitor can complement a primary perception stack by detecting persistent multimodal degradation, estimating conservative operational risk, and requesting reduced-speed or minimal-risk behavior before corrupted sensor streams produce unsafe control. The same design can be adapted to delivery robots, industrial mobile robots, shuttles, and other multimodal autonomous systems that must remain safe during partial sensor and communication failure.
```

## AMD Radeon and ROCm adoption

```text
Training and inference execute on an AMD Radeon gfx1100 GPU using PyTorch 2.9.1 with ROCm/HIP 7.2. The final model is trained on mixed MetaDrive and nuScenes temporal sequences and refuses silent CPU fallback. A real-data benchmark covers 30,142 held-out sequences from 130 nuScenes scenes at batch sizes 1, 32, 256, and 1024 in FP32 and ROCm AMP FP16. FP32 batch-1 p50 latency is 0.559 ms; batch-1024 throughput is 1.371 million sequences/s. AMP batch 1024 reduces p50 latency to 0.695 ms and peak allocated memory from 98.6 to 88.6 MiB. Across 8,192 real samples, AMP preserves 100% camera/LiDAR/timing classification agreement with FP32.
```

## Reproducibility statement

```text
All headline results follow frozen train/calibration/test partitions and deterministic seeds. nuScenes scenes are grouped by complete log_token to prevent adjacent-drive leakage. The MRM threshold is selected only on MetaDrive calibration seeds 113 and 127. All 20 external closed-loop seeds are reported without outcome-based selection. Model, dataset, and split hashes; per-run CSV files; aggregate JSON metrics; plots; and AMD/ROCm benchmark evidence are included or generated by documented commands.
```

## Responsible-use statement

```text
ChronoSafe-Drive is a research prototype, not a certified vehicle controller. The evaluated failures are controlled synthetic corruptions applied in simulation and to real logged sensor streams; they are not naturally annotated hardware failures. nuScenes is used under its official terms, and raw nuScenes media is not redistributed. The system must not be used as the sole safety mechanism for real-road deployment.
```
